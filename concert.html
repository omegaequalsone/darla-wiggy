As Darla

Concert & Opera

I started out as a classical singer. I really loved it and had some exceptional experiences — and I didn’t realize I was seeing some of the last glimpses of old-world glory.

i
So classy

The great diva Eva Marton as Turandot, only ten feet from me as I sang in the San Francisco Opera chorus. She insisted on wearing the traditional, original Met production costume instead of the David Hockney modern re-envisioning — she didn’t match the production, but a diva gets what a diva wants. One of my first glimpses of divadom.

There were incredibly lush productions like Samson and Delilah that were a joy — this was before productions became predominantly digital. You really felt immersed in a real, historical place: onstage with the great Olga Borodina, getting crushed under styrofoam rocks as the walls fell around us, Olga receiving ovations that lasted forever.

Or the extravagant Jean-Pierre Ponnelle production of Carmen, where I camped it up playing a small part during Denyce Graves’ debut in the title role — including a fight scene where she wrestled with me and carved a bloody X in my forehead. Don’t worry, it was food colouring. Even after she broke her foot in rehearsal she still performed the role and brought down the house.

ii
In the chorus

I’ve sung with both the San Francisco Opera and the San Francisco Symphony Chorus in over 200 productions, at the War Memorial Opera House, Davies Symphony Hall and Lincoln Center — conducted by Esa-Pekka Salonen, Michael Tilson Thomas, Kurt Masur, Donald Runnicles, Marin Alsop and many others.

I was what is called an “extra chorister” — part of operas that needed hundreds of people onstage. So I got a great view, usually from the back with a farm implement in my hand crying out in a Russian revolution, or as one of the party-goers in La Traviata in sumptuous costumes and wigs. The film about the chorus, In the Shadow of the Stars, really shows what dedication and skill that work takes. I loved being part of it — and I also wanted to explore my own voice as a soloist.

One of the best times I’ve had onstage was the Symphony Gala in 2019, with Nancy Pelosi, London Breed, Gavin Newsom and the cast of Beach Blanket Babylon onstage celebrating the great Michael Tilson Thomas. What a pleasure to contribute to the voices that celebrate San Francisco’s place as a cultural wonder, and a brave and proud celebrator of all people — especially those of us in the LGBTQ+ community.

iv
On the concert stage

In New York, singing with the New York Philharmonic as Kurt Masur conducted Carmina Burana during the Democratic Convention of 1994 — with Walter Cronkite and Yasser Arafat in the audience, the crowd leaping to its feet at the end in a glorious celebration of the power of great music. A memory I’ll treasure forever.

With the San Francisco Symphony, conducted by Michael Tilson Thomas, as Alto Soloist and Courtesan in Le Rossignol. Falla’s El Amor Brujo. Dvořák’s Stabat Mater with the Sacramento Choral Society. Mozart’s Requiem with the Sacramento Philharmonic, conducted by Michael Morgan. Vivaldi’s Gloria, Handel’s Messiah, and a chamber orchestra production of Handel’s Atalanta — plus concerts and recitals in California, Mississippi, Illinois, New York, Virginia, Vermont and Oregon.

v
Theatre & operetta

I’ve always loved acting, so musical theatre and operetta are some of my favourite places to be. One of my favourite performances was in Stephen Sondheim’s Follies at the stunning Paramount Theater in Oakland, with Michael Morgan conducting and fellow performers Rita Moreno, Sharon McNight and Val Diamond. There I met The Golden Follies, with whom I’ve performed so many times since. As we say — it was love at first sequin.

vi
New music

New music is so important, and some of the most extraordinary times I’ve had have been working on original pieces with new composers — at TheatreWorks (Jane Eyre), The Marsh, the SomArts Festival (Carla Lucero’s Suor Juana), and with The Friends and Enemies of New Music and American Opera Projects in New York.

Works include Rusty Magee’s Flurry Tales, performed at Lincoln Center; staged readings of The Mistress Cycle by Beth Blatt and Jennifer Gierling; and A Virtual Show with multimedia visionary George Coates — the first to merge live performers with computer-generated stage environments in real time, and a collaborator of Steve Jobs.

Festival Opera’s debut of Ned Rorem’s Our Town was another highlight. An idol of mine, the glorious Frederica von Stade, came backstage to congratulate Marnie Breckenridge, who was fantastic in the lead role. When Marnie introduced me, Flicka told me she thought I was terrific, and I thought I’d fall over from joy.

Another passion project was Girlkulture, created with Anja Strauss, Ariela Morgenstern and Lara Bruckmann — a classical cabaret performing Holländer, Weill, Spoliansky, Gershwin and Cole Porter at the Gunn Theater at the Legion of Honor, Cinnabar Theater, Old First Concerts and many club appearances.
